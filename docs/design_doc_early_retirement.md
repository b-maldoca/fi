# Design Document: Early Retirement Simulator (Zurich · Austria, Phase 1)

## 1. Overview
This document outlines the technical design for Phase 1 of the Zurich Early Retirement Simulator. Phase 1 focuses exclusively on the **post-retirement decumulation phase** for a single individual with no dependents living in Canton Zurich. 

The system will ingest initial asset balances, apply historic or Monte Carlo investment returns in CHF, compute complex Swiss tax obligations (including non-worker AHV contributions and capital withdrawal taxes), and visualize the resulting net worth trajectories.

> **Jurisdiction status:** §1–§6 describe the Swiss (Canton Zurich) system; **§7** describes the Austria jurisdiction behind the country switch (requirements in [PRD §8](prd_early_retirement_calc.md#8-austria-jurisdiction-country-switch)). Both are ✅ implemented and selectable in the app; the Austrian pension taxation verified against the official BMF Brutto-Netto-Rechner 2026 (13 pensions from €1,100 to €95,000 a month: health contribution, income tax and net pension within €0.13).

## 2. Architecture
To prioritize rapid development, rich interactive visualizations, and local data privacy, the application will be built using a **Python-based data science stack** and a **Streamlit** frontend.

*   **Frontend / UI**: Streamlit (Python). Runs locally, ensuring no sensitive financial data leaves the user's machine. Provides built-in interactive widgets and seamless integration with charting libraries.
*   **Core Engine**: Python 3.11+.
*   **Vectorization & Math**: `NumPy` and `Pandas` for running thousands of simulation iterations concurrently and efficiently.
*   **Visualization**: `Plotly` for interactive, performant web-based charts ("spaghetti" plots of individual paths, rendered for all historic cohorts and capped at the first 100 runs for Monte Carlo and Bootstrapping).
*   **Module Layout**: The engine lives in a regular Python package, `src/`, with an `__init__.py` that re-exports the public API. Intra-package imports are relative (`from .tax_engine import ...`); `app.py` and the tests import absolutely from the repository root (`from src.simulation_engine import ...`). No `sys.path` manipulation is used anywhere: Streamlit prepends the script's directory for `app.py`, and a root-level `conftest.py` makes pytest prepend the repository root for the test suite. Hot-reload during development is handled by Streamlit's own `LocalSourcesWatcher`, which evicts changed modules from `sys.modules` between reruns — no manual `importlib.reload` is required.
    *   `src/tax_engine.py` (Swiss tariffs, shared Zurich multiplier constants), `src/tax_engine_at.py` (Austrian 2026 tariff, KESt, Regelbesteuerungsoption, pension taxation, health self-insurance; §7.2), `src/jurisdiction.py` (country dispatch: `COUNTRY_DEFAULTS`, the `Jurisdiction` protocol and its `Switzerland` and `Austria` implementations, `indexed_tax`; see §7.2.1), `src/simulation_engine.py` (`SimConfig`, `run_simulation`, return generators, `historic_lognormal_params`), `src/historic_returns.py` (generated CHF monthly/annual data, return generators, `ppp_adjusted_annual_returns`), `src/historic_returns_eur.py` (generated EUR / Austria counterpart with Swiss-proxy flags; §7.5), and `src/metrics.py` (pure post-processing of simulation output: `cumulative_inflation`, `compute_success_mask`, `compute_survival_curves`, `first_depletion_year`, `first_shortfall_year`, `format_depletion_label`, `format_shortfall_label`, `funded_outflows` (expenses and taxes actually paid: `unfunded_deficit` deducted from expenses first, then taxes), `min_real_spending_pct` (on funded expenses), `min_real_net_worth`, `years_in_shortfall`, `real_final_net_worth`, `summarize_failure_timing`, `classify_outcome`, `classify_path_outcomes`, `worst_path_order`, `beginning_of_year_withdrawal_rate`, `RICH_MULTIPLE`, `OUTCOME_*` labels). `app.py` caches each of the three simulation pipelines independently via `@st.cache_data` (so post-processing threshold edits like `success_pct` or single-engine parameter changes do not recompute unaffected engines) and contains no success/TL;DR/survival/withdrawal-rate math of its own.
    *   Tests: `tests/test_tax_engine.py`, `tests/test_tax_engine_at.py`, `tests/test_jurisdiction.py`, `tests/test_austria_engine.py`, `tests/test_cost_basis.py`, `tests/test_historic_returns_eur.py`, `tests/test_simulation_engine.py`, `tests/test_metrics.py`, and `tests/test_app.py` (Streamlit `streamlit.testing.v1.AppTest` smoke tests running the full script headlessly for both countries; assertions use `at.main.*` because `at.header` also contains sidebar headers).

## 3. Core Modules

The system is divided into four primary modules:

### 3.1. User State & Configuration (Input Layer)
A centralized data structure (`SimConfig`) representing the simulation parameters, enforcing strict mathematical validation in `__post_init__`:
*   **Validation Rules**: `country in COUNTRY_DEFAULTS` (checked first; `"CH"` or `"AT"`), after which `bracket_indexation` / `cash_rate` left at `None` resolve to the country defaults; for `"AT"`, positive Pillar 2 / 3a balances are rejected and `health_insurance_monthly` must be ≥ 0; integer type check (rejecting `bool` and floats) on `num_runs`, `duration_years`, `start_age`, `tent_duration_years`, and `seed`; `np.isfinite` check (rejecting `NaN` and `±inf`) across all numeric fields and `initial_pillar_3a_accounts`; `num_runs > 0`, `duration_years > 0`, `start_age >= 0`, `tent_duration_years >= 0`, `inflation_std >= 0.0`, `inflation_mean > -1.0`, `cash_rate > -1.0`, `seed >= 0`, `real_chf_appreciation < 1.0`, `-1.0 <= btc_equity_corr <= 1.0`, `bracket_indexation >= 0.0`, `spending_strategy in VALID_SPENDING_STRATEGIES`, `rebalance_strategy in VALID_REBALANCE_STRATEGIES`, individual asset allocations $\ge 0.0$ and $\sum \text{allocations} = 1.0$, non-negative initial asset balances (`initial_liquid_wealth`, `initial_pillar_2`, `initial_pillar_3a_accounts`), non-negative expenses/income/yields (`annual_base_expenses`, `monthly_ahv_pension`, `dividend_yield`), non-negative tax multipliers (`cantonal_multiplier`, `municipal_multiplier`), non-negative rebalancing thresholds, non-negative spending strategy parameters (`vanguard_target_rate`, `vanguard_floor_pct`, `vanguard_ceiling_pct`, `dynamic_expense_floor_pct`, `dynamic_expense_ceiling_pct`), `dynamic_expense_floor_pct <= dynamic_expense_ceiling_pct` when `spending_strategy == "Dynamic (Floor & Ceiling)"`, and `vanguard_floor_pct <= 1.0` (a cut above 100% would permit negative spending). The Streamlit widgets carry matching `min_value`/`max_value` bounds so invalid UI input never reaches `__post_init__`.
*   **Normalization**: `initial_pillar_3a_accounts` defaults to a fresh empty list (`field(default_factory=list)`; an explicit `None` is also coerced to `[]`), and the legacy `enable_dynamic_expenses=True` flag coerces `spending_strategy` from `"Static"` to `"Dynamic (Floor & Ceiling)"` before strategy validation runs.
*   **Demographics**: Retirement age (`start_age`), simulation duration (`duration_years`).
*   **Economics & Taxes**: Inflation mean & volatility (for Monte Carlo), dividend yield, Year 0 cash interest rate (`cash_rate`, `None` → country default: `0.01` in CH, `0.02` in AT), Bitcoin–equity monthly log-return correlation (`btc_equity_corr`, default `0.50`), cantonal tax multiplier (default `ZURICH_CANTONAL_MULTIPLIER = 0.95`, Canton Zurich 2026), municipal tax multiplier (Steuerfuss, default `ZURICH_CITY_MUNICIPAL_MULTIPLIER = 1.19`, Zurich City), **Real CHF / EUR Appreciation beyond PPP** (`real_chf_appreciation`, default `0.0` = Relative PPP neutrality; `HISTORIC_REAL_CHF_APPRECIATION` ≈ `0.0071` / `HISTORIC_REAL_EUR_APPRECIATION` ≈ `0.0057` = raw 1922–2025 history), and **Tax Bracket Indexation** (`bracket_indexation`, `None` → country default: `1.0` in CH per Art. 39 DBG / § 48 StG ZH, `2/3` in AT per § 33a EStG).
*   **Assets**: Taxable Liquid Wealth, Pillar 2 (`Freizügigkeitskonto`), Pillar 3a balances (`initial_pillar_3a_accounts`, configured in the UI via `Number of Pillar 3a Accounts` (`0–5`) and a single `Balance per Pillar 3a Account (CHF)` input applied equally across all accounts: `[pillar_3a_balance] * int(num_pillar_3a)`).
*   **Expenses & Income**: Annual base expenses, Spending Strategy (`Static`, `Dynamic (Floor & Ceiling)`, `Vanguard Dynamic`), and Expected **Monthly** Pillar 1 (AHV) Pension from age 65.
*   **Simulation Config**: Simulation Mode (`Historic Backtesting`, `Historic Bootstrapping`, `Monte Carlo`), Number of Runs (`mc_num_runs`, `boot_num_runs`), Stationary Bootstrap Mean Block Length (`block_size_years`, default `5`), **Random Seed** (`random_seed`, default `42`), and Success Criteria (`success_pct` of inflation-adjusted starting net worth).

### 3.2. Tax & Pension Engine
This is a stateless utility module (`src/tax_engine.py`) responsible for all Swiss-specific tax and social security calculations for a given year. The simulation engine never calls it directly: the Swiss rules that combine these tariffs (Year-0 estimates, pension liquidations, latent withdrawal tax, year-end assessment) live in the `Switzerland` class of `src/jurisdiction.py` (§7.2.1). All functions are vectorized, support both scalar and N-dimensional array inputs while preserving return type and shape, and safely clamp negative inputs to zero.

*   **Bracket Table Vintages & Verification**: `FEDERAL_INCOME_BRACKETS` (2026, Art. 36 Abs. 1 DBG, single: 15,200 / 33,200 / 43,500 / 58,000 / 76,200 / 82,100 / 108,900 / 141,500 / 185,100), `ZURICH_INCOME_BRACKETS` (2026, § 35 Abs. 1 StG ZH Grundtarif, base rate: 7,000 / 12,000 / 16,800 / 24,800 / 34,500 / 45,700 / 58,800 / 76,400 / 110,400 / 144,100 / 197,400 / 266,700 at 2%…13%), `ZURICH_WEALTH_BRACKETS` (2026, § 47 StG ZH, base rate: 80,000 / 321,000 / 727,000 / 1,371,000 / 2,339,000 / 3,304,000 at 0.5‰…3‰), and the AHV non-worker table (530 CHF min / 26,500 CHF max, unchanged for 2026). The three tax tables were reverse-engineered from ~1,800 queries to the official ESTV calculator API (`swisstaxcalculator.estv.admin.ch`, Zurich City, single, no church) and are pinned by golden tests (`test_income_tax_matches_estv_2026`, `test_wealth_tax_matches_estv_2026`, `test_capital_withdrawal_tax_matches_estv_2026`); the model matches within CHF 1–2. Deliberately not modelled (each < CHF 25/yr and would break positive homogeneity): flooring of taxable amounts to CHF 100 / 1,000, the Art. 36 Abs. 3 DBG CHF 25 minimum, and the CHF 24 Zurich personal tax. These are the *base-year* tables; the simulation engine indexes them forward each year at runtime (see **Bracket Indexation** below) rather than storing pre-inflated copies. The 2026 Zurich City multipliers are defined once as `ZURICH_CANTONAL_MULTIPLIER = 0.95` and `ZURICH_CITY_MUNICIPAL_MULTIPLIER = 1.19` and used as the defaults of every tax function, `SimConfig`, and the UI.
*   **Scalar/array handling**: the private helpers `_is_scalar` / `_as_output` implement the "scalar in → float out, array in → array out" contract once for all public functions.

*   **Income Tax (`calculate_income_tax(taxable_income, cantonal_multiplier=0.95, municipal_multiplier=1.19)`)**:
    *   Applies Federal progressive brackets (Single tariff), capped at the constitutional maximum `min(tariff(x), 0.115 * x)` (Art. 128 BV) in `_federal_income_tariff`.
    *   Applies Zurich Cantonal progressive base brackets (Single tariff) multiplied by `cantonal_multiplier + municipal_multiplier` (e.g., `0.95 + 1.19 = 2.14` for Zurich City 2026).
*   **Wealth Tax (`calculate_wealth_tax(taxable_wealth, cantonal_multiplier=0.95, municipal_multiplier=1.19)`)**:
    *   Applies Zurich Cantonal progressive base wealth tax brackets multiplied by `cantonal_multiplier + municipal_multiplier`. Assessed on year-end liquid wealth after deducting the current year's living expenses (`max(0, total_liquid_end - current_expenses)`).
*   **Capital Withdrawal Tax (`calculate_capital_withdrawal_tax(amount, cantonal_multiplier=0.95, municipal_multiplier=1.19)`)**:
    *   Applied immediately at source when Pillar 2 or Pillar 3a accounts are liquidated.
    *   **Federal** (Art. 38 DBG): `federal_income_tariff(C) / 5` (inherits the 11.5% cap).
    *   **Zurich** (§ 37 StG ZH, since 2022): the rate for a notional annual pension of `C / 20` is applied to the full amount, with a 2% minimum simple tax: `simple = max(0.02 * C, 20 * T_ZH(C / 20))`, then `simple * (cantonal_multiplier + municipal_multiplier)`. (The previous `T_ZH(C) / 10` approximation understated the tax by ~2.6× at CHF 1M and ignored the 2% floor.)
    *   All-in Zurich City reference points: CHF 100k → 4.80% (floor binds), CHF 1M → ≈ 10.9%.
    *   Both parts remain positively homogeneous of degree 1 in the bracket edges, so the indexation identity below stays exact.
*   **AHV Non-Worker Contributions (`calculate_ahv_non_worker(wealth, imputed_pension_income=0)`)**:
    *   Calculated for early retirees (`current_age < 65`) based on the official 2025/2026 AHV tables: `determining_wealth = wealth + 20 * imputed_pension_income` (where `wealth` is `taxable_wealth`). Contribution is 530 CHF for wealth < 350k CHF. For wealth between 350k and 1.75M CHF, it adds 106 CHF for every 50k CHF step above 300k CHF. For wealth above 1.75M CHF, it adds 159 CHF for every 50k CHF step above 1.75M CHF. Capped at 26,500 CHF/year. Under **Art. 33 Abs. 1 lit. d DBG** and **§ 31 Abs. 1 lit. d StG ZH**, mandatory non-worker AHV/IV/EO contributions are fully deductible from taxable income (`taxable_income = max(0, dividends + interest + annual_ahv_received - ahv_contrib)`).
*   **Bracket Indexation (`SimConfig.bracket_indexation`, `_indexed_tax` in `src/simulation_engine.py`)**:
    *   Swiss law indexes franc-denominated tax brackets to the CPI so that purely nominal growth does not push a taxpayer into higher brackets ("kalte Progression"). **Art. 39 DBG** requires the EFD to adjust the federal tariff steps and franc-denominated deductions **annually** to the LIK (index level at 30 June preceding the tax period, rounded to CHF 100), and **§ 48 StG ZH** indexes the Canton Zurich tariff steps and deductions for **both income and wealth tax**. The AHV non-worker scale tracks the Mischindex (mean of wage and price indices) roughly every two years, so CPI indexation of that table is a slight under-adjustment.
    *   `bracket_indexation` is the fraction of each year's realized inflation passed through to the bracket edges: `1.0` (default) reproduces the legal regime, `0.0` freezes the brackets in nominal terms so the retiree absorbs the full bracket creep, and intermediate values model delayed or partially suspended cantonal compensation. The engine compounds an `indexed_cpi_level` array (`*= np.maximum(0.01, 1 + bracket_indexation * inflation)`) in lockstep with `inflation_factors` and derives `bracket_factors` from it through `Jurisdiction.next_bracket_factors`, because **no jurisdiction lowers its brackets in deflation**: Switzerland (Art. 39 Abs. 2 DBG, § 48 StG ZH) uses the high-water mark `max(bracket_factors, indexed_cpi_level)` (no downward adjustment; the next adjustment is measured from the last one), Austria (§ 33a EStG) `bracket_factors × (1 + bracket_indexation × max(0, inflation))` (negative years skipped, never clawed back).
    *   Indexation is applied via the homogeneity identity `T_indexed(x, f) == f * T_nominal(x / f)`, which is exact for every tariff in `tax_engine`: the income and wealth tariffs are piecewise linear, and the AHV step function scales correctly because floor division is scale invariant (`(f*a) // (f*b) == a // b`). This keeps `tax_engine` the single source of truth for the bracket tables rather than maintaining a second, inflated copy. Bracket *rates* and the Steuerfuss multipliers are deliberately **not** scaled, since both are political parameters.
    *   *Note: Indexation removes only the spurious, inflation-driven bracket creep. A portfolio compounding faster than the CPI still climbs the wealth tax schedule in real terms at any setting, which is economically correct and remains one of the dominant drags in the model.*
*   **Pillar 1 AHV Pension Payments**:
    *   Starting at age 65, the user receives a monthly AHV pension (`monthly_ahv_pension * inflation_factors`), added directly to CHF Cash each month. The input value (in today's CHF) compounds with annual CPI inflation from Year 0 (including all years prior to age 65).
    *   Annual AHV payouts (`12 * monthly_ahv_pension * inflation_factors`) are included in annual taxable income.

### 3.3. Simulation Engine (The Core Loop)
The engine (`run_simulation` in `src/simulation_engine.py`) resolves `jurisdiction = get_jurisdiction(config.country)` once, tracks an average-cost basis for every sleeve alongside the balances (every purchase, sale and rebalance is booked against it; see §7.4.1), and delegates every country-specific step (Step 1 liquidations, watermark latent tax, Step 6 tax assessment) to it, then validates that `return_matrix.shape == (num_runs, duration_years * 12, 5)` and (when provided) `inflation_matrix.shape == (num_runs, duration_years)`, and executes a **monthly tick** (`m in range(duration_years * 12)`) for `num_runs` paths simultaneously using NumPy arrays across 5 liquid asset classes (`0: US Stocks`, `1: Non-US Stocks`, `2: CHF Cash`, `3: Gold`, `4: Bitcoin`).

**Monthly Tick Execution Order:**
1. **Start-of-Year Pension Liquidations & Immediate Capital Withdrawal Tax (Month 0 of each year)**:
    * **Age < 65**: Staggered liquidation of Pillar 3a accounts at ages `60 + i` (`60, 61, 62, 63, 64` for up to 5 accounts, at most 1 account liquidated per year).
    * **Age $\ge 65$**: All remaining Pillar 3a accounts and the Pillar 2 (`Freizügigkeitskonto`) account are liquidated (if starting retirement at age $\ge 65$, this occurs immediately in Year 0 Month 0).
    * **At-Source Tax Deduction & Debt Repayment**: `calculate_capital_withdrawal_tax` is computed on the total pension liquidation for the year using start-of-year `bracket_factors` (so Day-1 liquidations at `year == 0` use uninflated `f = 1.0`, matching `_get_year_0_liquid_wealth` and `initial_net_worth_after_tax`) and deducted immediately at source. Net proceeds pay off any negative CHF Cash balance from prior bridge deficits first so solvent portfolios never carry involuntary 5% margin debt alongside leveraged equities; any remaining surplus is invested into `liquid_assets` proportionally to `current_target_weights`.
2. **Annual Inflation & Bracket Indexation Update (Month 0 of each year)**:
    * Updates cumulative `inflation_factors *= np.maximum(0.01, 1.0 + inflation)` using `inflation_matrix[:, year]` (or sampling from a deterministic `np.random.default_rng(config.seed + 10_000)` stream if `inflation_matrix` is `None`). Clamping `1.0 + inflation >= 0.01` prevents division-by-zero or sign inversion under extreme synthetic deflation draws.
    * Updates a parallel `indexed_cpi_level *= np.maximum(0.01, 1.0 + config.bracket_indexation * inflation)` and sets `bracket_factors = jurisdiction.next_bracket_factors(bracket_factors, indexed_cpi_level, inflation, config.bracket_indexation)`, which scales all currency-denominated tax bracket edges for the year (see §3.2). The factor never decreases: CH takes the high-water mark of `indexed_cpi_level` (at `bracket_indexation = 1.0` it tracks `inflation_factors` exactly until the first deflation year, then stays at the previous peak until the CPI level exceeds it), AT skips negative inflation. At `0.0` it stays pinned at `1.0`.
3. **Market Returns & Bankruptcy Handling (Every Month)**:
    * Remaining Pillar 2 and Pillar 3a balances grow by 100% equity monthly returns weighted by the user's US vs. Non-US stock target allocation (or 50/50 if target equity weight is zero, floored at `-100%`).
    * **Bankruptcy Check**: If total liquid wealth is negative (`total_liquid < 0`), all negative debt is consolidated into CHF Cash (index 2) and other liquid asset classes are zeroed.
    * Positive liquid asset balances earn their respective monthly market returns (`return_matrix[:, m, :]`); negative balances incur a 5% APY debt interest penalty (`(1.05)**(1/12) - 1`).
4. **Monthly AHV Pension Addition (Every Month if `current_age >= 65`)**:
    * Adds `monthly_ahv_pension * inflation_factors` directly to `liquid_assets[:, 2]` (CHF Cash).
5. **Intra-Year Rebalancing Check (Months 0..10; Month 11 rebalances after Step 6)**:
    * Triggered in months `0..10` every month (`Monthly`), at quarter-end (`Quarterly`, `month_of_year % 3 == 2`), or whenever max absolute weight drift exceeds `rebalance_threshold` (`Threshold`), targeting `current_target_weights = target_weights_by_year[year]`.
    * Only solvent portfolios (`total_liquid_val > 0`) are rebalanced, and `_deferred_withdrawal_tax` is evaluated only when `enable_smart_selling` is active *and* at least one run has `do_rebalance == True`.
    * **Cash Tent (`get_target_weights` & `estimate_year_0_taxes`)**: Peak target cash weight at Year 0 is computed as `min(1.0, tent_duration_years * (annual_base_expenses + estimate_year_0_taxes(config)) / init_wealth)` (where `estimate_year_0_taxes` solves a 2-step fixed point on Year 0's actual Cash Tent weights when `rebalance_strategy == "Cash Tent"`), and glides linearly down to `alloc_chf_cash` over `tent_duration_years` (default: 7 years). Non-cash weights are rescaled by `(1 - cash_weight) / (1 - alloc_chf_cash)` so the vector always sums to 1.0. The glidepath is bypassed and flat `base_weights` are returned whenever `tent_duration_years <= 0`, `year >= tent_duration_years`, `init_wealth <= 0`, `alloc_chf_cash >= 1.0`, or `w_peak <= alloc_chf_cash`.
    * **Per-year precompute & timing**: `run_simulation` evaluates `get_target_weights` **once per year** before the monthly loop (`target_weights_by_year`). `current_target_weights = target_weights_by_year[year]` drives pension-proceeds allocation and intra-year rebalancing, while the Month-11 year-end rebalance (Step 7, *after* Step 6's tax/expense assessment and deduction) targets `target_weights_by_year[year + 1]` (the final year keeps its own weights), so Year *y* is taxed on the allocation actually held during Year *y* and base weights are reached after exactly `tent_duration_years`.
    * **Smart Cash Buffer**: If `enable_smart_selling` is enabled, rebalancing is skipped for any run currently in a market downturn (`current_nw_after_tax < initial_net_worth_after_tax * inflation_factors`).
    * **After-tax watermark (`_deferred_withdrawal_tax`)**: every watermark comparison (Smart Cash Buffer, Dynamic Floor & Ceiling, `below_watermark`) uses net worth minus the capital withdrawal tax still owed on the pension balances. `_deferred_withdrawal_tax(pillar_2, pillar_3a, from_age, bracket_factors, config)` replays the liquidation schedule from `from_age` on current balances (no growth): before 65 at most one non-empty 3a account per year (first account with `60 + i <= age`), then all remaining 3a accounts plus Pillar 2 as one event at 65; each event is taxed with `_indexed_tax(calculate_capital_withdrawal_tax, …)`. Inside the loop `from_age = current_age + 1` (this year's month-0 liquidation already ran); the starting base `initial_net_worth_after_tax` uses `from_age = start_age` and unit bracket factors. Vanguard Dynamic's `target_exp` and the success criterion still use gross net worth.
6. **Annual Taxation, Spending & Outflow Deduction (Month 11 of each year)**:
    * **Spending Evaluation**:
        * **Static**: `current_expenses = annual_base_expenses * inflation_factors`.
        * **Dynamic (Floor & Ceiling)**: Scales inflation-adjusted base expenses by `dynamic_expense_floor_pct` when after-tax net worth is below the inflation-adjusted starting watermark, and by `dynamic_expense_ceiling_pct` when above.
        * **Vanguard Dynamic**: Recalculates target net living expenses as `vanguard_target_rate * max(0, current_nw_before_expenses)`, clamped between `(1 - vanguard_floor_pct)` and `(1 + vanguard_ceiling_pct)` of the prior year's inflation-adjusted living expenses (initializing `vanguard_prev_expenses` to `vanguard_target_rate * initial_net_worth` if `annual_base_expenses == 0` for programmatic callers). In `app.py`, the UI's tax-inclusive Target Withdrawal Rate ($\text{TWR}$) is bi-directionally synchronized with Year 0 Annual Base Expenses ($E_0$) via fixed-point iteration solving $E_0 + \text{Taxes}_0(E_0) = \text{TWR} \times \text{NetWorth}_0$ using live user tax/yield/Cash-Tent inputs, and converted to the net living expense rate `vanguard_target_rate = E_0 / NetWorth_0` for `SimConfig` so taxes are never double-counted.
    * **Tax Assessment**: Computes `wealth_tax` and `ahv_contrib` (for `current_age < 65`) on `taxable_wealth = max(0, total_liquid_end - current_expenses)`, then computes annual `income_tax` on `taxable_income = np.maximum(0.0, dividends + interest + annual_ahv_received - ahv_contrib)` — where `dividends = (US Stocks + Non-US Stocks) * dividend_yield` and `interest = CHF Cash * np.maximum(0.0, realized_cash_ret)` (`realized_cash_ret = np.prod(1.0 + return_matrix[:, (m - 11):(m + 1), 2], axis=1) - 1.0`) are evaluated on the pre-rebalance holdings held during the year, and `ahv_contrib` is deducted per Art. 33 Abs. 1 lit. d DBG / § 31 Abs. 1 lit. d StG ZH.
    * **Asset Selling**: Deducts `deficit = current_expenses + total_taxes`. Runs in `cash_first` spend positive cash first and sell the remainder proportionally: Smart Cash Buffer runs in a downturn (`enable_smart_selling` and below the watermark) and, where the jurisdiction taxes capital gains (Austria), every run under a strategy in `YEAR_END_REBALANCE_STRATEGIES` (`Monthly`, `Quarterly`, `Yearly`, `Cash Tent`), because the year-end rebalance right after the sale restores the target weights anyway (selling proportionally and buying back with the leftover cash would realize taxable gains for nothing). Otherwise, all positive liquid assets are sold proportionally to their current holdings; any unpaid deficit beyond total liquid assets becomes negative CHF Cash (and is recorded in `unfunded_deficit`).
7. **Year-End Rebalancing (Month 11, post-outflow)**:
    * For solvent portfolios (`post_sale_liquid > 0`, and not in a downturn when `enable_smart_selling` is enabled), rebalances `liquid_assets` to `rebalance_target_weights` (`target_weights_by_year[year + 1]` if `year + 1 < duration_years` else `current_target_weights`) under `Monthly`, `Quarterly`, `Yearly`, `Cash Tent`, or drifted `Threshold`.

### 3.4. Return Generators & Data Provenance
The system provides a two-stage reproducible data pipeline (`scripts/fetch_source_data.py` $\to$ `data/*.csv` $\to$ `scripts/build_historic_returns.py` $\to$ `src/historic_returns.py`) covering 1922–2025 (`104` years, `1,248` monthly observations). The same build script with `--currency EUR` (after `scripts/fetch_source_data_at.py`) generates the Austrian counterpart `src/historic_returns_eur.py` (§7.5); everything below describes the CHF build:
*   **Empirical Source Datasets (`data/`)**:
    *   `us_stocks.csv`: True **month-end** S&P 500 Total Return index in USD (1871–2025, via [wichtounet/swr-calculator](https://github.com/wichtounet/swr-calculator); verified to $\pm 0.01\text{pp}$ against published month-end S&P 500 total returns). Carried as **real monthly returns** (`MONTHLY_US_CHF`) throughout 1922–2025.
    *   `jst_exus_usd.csv` + `ex_us_stocks.csv`: Spliced Non-US Equities total return series. Because `ex_us_stocks.csv` is byte-identical to `us_stocks.csv` prior to Dec 1969, **1922–1969** uses a GDP-weighted 17-country ex-US total return index constructed from the **Jordà–Schularick–Taylor Macrohistory Database R6** (`eq_tr` converted to USD via `xrusd`, then to CHF; annual returns expanded into 12 geometric monthly returns, flagged in `MONTHLY_EXUS_IS_SMOOTHED`), spliced with **real monthly** MSCI EAFE / World ex-US returns (`ex_us_stocks.csv`) for **1970–2025** (`MONTHLY_NON_US_CHF`).
    *   `usd_chf.csv`: True **month-end** USD/CHF exchange rate from **FRED `DEXSZUS`** daily observations for **1971–present** (replacing a legacy hand-extended 2024–2025 tail that overstated Dec 2025 USD/CHF by 13.4%), spliced with **SNB `devkum`** monthly averages for **1914–1970** (Bretton Woods / gold-standard peg era).
    *   `ch_inflation.csv`: Historical monthly Swiss Consumer Price Index (CPI / LIK) from the Swiss Federal Statistical Office (FSO/BFS, 1921–present).
    *   `gold_usd.csv`: **LBMA London PM Fix** month-end USD/oz prices from **April 1968–present** multiplied by `usd_chf.csv` to yield **real monthly CHF gold returns** (`MONTHLY_GOLD_CHF`), with pre-1968 statutory fixed USD prices (`$20.67` then `$35.00`) reflecting currency devaluations smoothed across the year (`MONTHLY_GOLD_IS_SMOOTHED`).
    *   `ch_cash_rate.csv`: Annual CHF cash yield (`1900–2025`). `retail_rate` (used by the simulation) = **SNB private-client savings-deposit rate** (data.snb.ch cube `zikrepro`, `D0=M`, `D1=S1`, annual mean of monthly observations, full years only, asserted non-negative) from **1933**, and **JST R6** Swiss `bill_rate` for 1900–1932 (0.007pp mean abs diff to the SNB savings rate on the 1933–1968 overlap). `wholesale_rate` (reference only) = JST `bill_rate` 1900–2020 + SNB policy / SARON 2021–2025. No 0% floor is applied (the savings rate never went negative). `MONTHLY_CASH_CHF`, `HISTORIC_RETURNS_CASH_CHF`, 1922–2025: `2.49%` nominal CAGR, `1.42%` vol, `+0.74%` real after `1.74%` Swiss CPI.
    *   `us_cpi.csv`: Monthly US CPI-U — **FRED `CPIAUCNS`** from 1913-01, legacy swr-calculator leg for 1871–1912 (spliced by `scripts/fetch_source_data.py`, which reports the legacy-vs-BLS overlap deviation, max 0.74%). **Build-time only** — consumed by `scripts/build_historic_returns.py` to derive the PPP constant; never loaded by the simulation.
*   **Build-time integrity checks**: the Gold series is asserted to be gap-free across the window (no silent `fillna(0.0)`), and `HISTORIC_REAL_CHF_APPRECIATION` is recomputed on every build from the geometric 1922–2025 drifts: $g_{\text{FX}}$ (USD/CHF) and $g_{\text{PPP}} = \frac{1 + \pi_{\text{CH}}}{1 + \pi_{\text{US}}} - 1$, giving $a_{\text{hist}} = \text{round}\left(1 - \frac{1 + g_{\text{FX}}}{1 + g_{\text{PPP}}}, 4\right)$ = `0.0071`. `tests/test_simulation_engine.py::test_historic_real_chf_appreciation_is_derived_from_data` recomputes it from the CSVs.
*   **Currency / PPP Adjustment (`_apply_fx_ppp_adjustment`, `ppp_adjusted_annual_returns`, `HISTORIC_REAL_CHF_APPRECIATION = 0.0071`)**: The CHF has appreciated ≈ `0.71%/yr` in real terms beyond Relative PPP over 1922–2025, i.e. a real FX drag on foreign-priced assets. When `real_chf_appreciation` is passed (UI default `0.0`), foreign-priced sleeves (`ASSET_US_STOCKS`, `ASSET_NON_US_STOCKS`, `ASSET_GOLD`) are multiplicatively scaled each month by $\text{month\_adj} = \left(\frac{1 - \text{real\_chf\_appreciation}}{1 - a_{\text{hist}}}\right)^{1/12}$ via $r_m^{\text{adj}} = (1 + r_m)\cdot\text{month\_adj} - 1$. The annual counterpart `ppp_adjusted_annual_returns(annual_returns, real_chf_appreciation) = (1 + R)\cdot\text{month\_adj}^{12} - 1` is exactly what compounding the twelve adjusted months yields (pinned by `test_ppp_adjusted_annual_returns_matches_monthly_engine_adjustment`); `app.py` feeds it into `historic_lognormal_params` so the Monte Carlo default means for the same three sleeves embed the same FX assumption as the historic engines (annual log-volatility is invariant to the rescaling). Because the defaults depend on the slider, the **Currency / PPP Assumption** section is rendered *before* **Monte Carlo Parameters** in the sidebar.
*   **Gaussian Copula Bitcoin–Equity Coupling (`_generate_lognormal_monthly_returns`, `BITCOIN_NOMINAL_MEAN = 0.07`, `BITCOIN_VOL = 0.50`, `DEFAULT_BTC_EQUITY_CORR = 0.50`)**: Full-sample standardized monthly log-returns of `MONTHLY_US_CHF` ($Z_{\text{US}, t} = (\ln(1 + R_{\text{US}, t}) - \mu_{\text{US,1922–2025}})/\sigma_{\text{US,1922–2025}}$, ensuring unconditional $\mathbb{E}[Z_{\text{US}}] = 0$ and $\text{Std}(Z_{\text{US}}) = 1$ so secular US drift never leaks into Bitcoin's expected log-return while avoiding per-path sum-to-zero variance collapse and look-ahead bias) are combined with an independent standard normal draw $\varepsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ via $Z_{\text{BTC}, t} = \rho Z_{\text{US}, t} + \sqrt{1 - \rho^2}\varepsilon_t$ before applying the Ito-corrected lognormal map (`btc_mean`, default `7%`, and `btc_vol`, default `50%`). Cohort RNG streams in `get_historic_return_matrix` use `np.random.SeedSequence([seed, 1, i])` so cohorts never collide with Monte Carlo or shifted seeds.
*   **Historic Backtesting Mode (`get_historic_return_matrix`, `get_historic_inflation_matrix`, `compute_effective_sample_size`)**: Replays contiguous historical monthly returns (`MONTHLY_US_CHF`, `MONTHLY_NON_US_CHF`, `MONTHLY_CASH_CHF`, `MONTHLY_GOLD_CHF`, adjusted for `real_chf_appreciation`) and annual Swiss CPI inflation (`HISTORIC_SWISS_INFLATION`) across 1922–2025 (`104` years, $N = \text{total\_years} - \text{duration\_years} + 1$ cohorts), paired with Gaussian-copula Bitcoin returns (`btc_mean`, `btc_vol`, `btc_equity_corr`). `compute_effective_sample_size` calculates the non-overlapping sample size $N_{\text{eff}} = T / D$ and the two-sided 90% Wilson confidence interval ($z = 1.64485$).
*   **Historic Bootstrapping Mode (`generate_bootstrapped_data`, `POLITIS_WHITE_BLOCK_YEARS = 5`)**: Implements the **Politis–Romano (1994) Stationary Bootstrap** (`stationary=True` default) with **circular wrap-around** `(year_idx[:, t - 1] + 1) % total_years` and geometric block lengths of mean $b = \text{block\_size\_years}$ (at each year $t > 0$, starting a new random year with probability $p = 1/b$ and continuing the contiguous historical sequence with probability $1 - 1/b$). Setting `stationary=False` falls back to fixed non-circular `block_size_years` block sampling. Jointly resamples `MONTHLY_US_CHF`, `MONTHLY_NON_US_CHF`, `MONTHLY_CASH_CHF`, `MONTHLY_GOLD_CHF`, and `HISTORIC_SWISS_INFLATION` with Gaussian-copula Bitcoin returns (`btc_mean`, `btc_vol`, `btc_equity_corr`).
*   **Parametric Monte Carlo Mode (`generate_monte_carlo_returns`, `generate_monte_carlo_inflation`)**: Generates an asset return matrix of `shape=(num_runs, duration_months, 5)` seeded by `random_seed` using a lognormal model with Ito drift correction ($\text{drift} = \ln(1 + R) - 0.5\sigma^2$) and correlated monthly shocks (`US`/`Non-US` $\rho=0.75$, `Gold` $\rho=0.08$, `Bitcoin` $\rho=\text{btc\_equity\_corr}$) for US Stocks, Non-US Stocks, Gold, and Bitcoin, constant geometric monthly return $(1 + R_{\text{cash}})^{1/12} - 1$ for CHF Cash, and independent normally distributed annual inflation `generate_monte_carlo_inflation` seeded at `random_seed + 10_000`. **Parameterisation & calibration (`historic_lognormal_params`)**: `ann_ret` is the *arithmetic* mean of annual simple returns ($E[1+R] = 1 + \text{ann\_ret}$) and `ann_vol` is the standard deviation of annual *log* returns; `historic_lognormal_params(annual_returns) -> (mean, log_vol)` is the exact inverse and is used by the UI to default US Mean, Non-US Mean, Equities Volatility (average of US and ex-US log-vol, ≈ 21.0%), Gold Mean and Gold Volatility (≈ 15.3%) to the 1922–2025 CHF history, while Inflation Mean (≈ 1.8%) and Inflation Volatility (≈ 3.6%) default to the sample mean and std of `HISTORIC_SWISS_INFLATION`. The three foreign-priced means are computed on `ppp_adjusted_annual_returns(series, real_chf_appreciation)` rather than on the raw series, so they move with the **Currency / PPP Assumption** slider exactly as the historic engines do (default `0.00%`: US ≈ 12.1%, Non-US ≈ 9.3%, Gold ≈ 5.5%; at `+0.71%`: US ≈ 11.3%, Non-US ≈ 8.6%, Gold ≈ 4.7%; log-vols unchanged). Only CHF cash stays a forward-looking default (1%, also used as the Year 0 taxable savings rate); historic values are shown in every input's help text.

*   `net_worth`: Annual total net worth array `shape=(duration_years, num_runs)`
*   `liquid_assets`: Annual taxable liquid assets array `shape=(duration_years, num_runs)`
*   `liquid_assets_by_class`: Annual asset class breakdown `shape=(duration_years, num_runs, 5)`
*   `pillar_2`: Annual Pillar 2 balance `shape=(duration_years, num_runs)`
*   `pillar_3a`: Annual total Pillar 3a balance `shape=(duration_years, num_runs)`
*   `taxes_paid`: Annual taxes and mandatory contributions paid `shape=(duration_years, num_runs)` — CH: income + wealth + AHV non-worker + capital withdrawal tax; AT: income tax + KESt + health insurance (6% on the pension and § 16 ASVG self-insurance)
*   `expenses_paid`: Annual living expenses target `shape=(duration_years, num_runs)`
*   `income_dividends`: Annual dividend income `shape=(duration_years, num_runs)`
*   `income_interest`: Annual taxable cash interest (CHF or EUR) `shape=(duration_years, num_runs)`
*   `income_ahv`: Annual gross state pension received (CH: 12 AHV payments; AT: 14 payments) `shape=(duration_years, num_runs)`
*   `unfunded_deficit`: Annual unpaid deficit beyond positive liquid assets `shape=(duration_years, num_runs)`
*   `below_watermark`: Boolean mask indicating runs whose *after-tax* net worth is below the inflation-adjusted after-tax starting watermark `shape=(duration_years, num_runs)`
*   `cost_basis_by_class`: Year-end average-cost basis per asset class `shape=(duration_years, num_runs, 5)` (cash always at cost; §7.4.1)
*   `realized_gains`: Annual realized capital gains (negative = net loss) summed over the non-cash sleeves `shape=(duration_years, num_runs)`. Tracked in both countries, not taxed in Switzerland. Includes the year-end rebalance, whose gains Austria taxes in the following year's assessment (§7.4.2), so a year's value can differ from the gains taxed that year.
*   `unrealized_gains`: Year-end unrealized gains (`value − basis`) summed over the non-cash sleeves `shape=(duration_years, num_runs)`
*   `tax_components`: dict of annual tax components `shape=(duration_years, num_runs)` each — CH: `income_tax`, `wealth_tax`, `ahv_contributions`; AT: `income_tax`, `kest`, `health_insurance` (§7.4.2)
*   `initial_net_worth`: Float scalar starting net worth (gross; used by the success criterion)
*   `initial_net_worth_after_tax`: Float scalar starting net worth minus latent Day-0 taxes (deferred capital withdrawal tax on initial pension balances in CH; equals `initial_net_worth` in AT since Day-0 unrealized gains are zero)

### 7.1. Approach
*   **One app, one engine** (agreed): a `country` field on `SimConfig` selects the jurisdiction. Country-specific logic is confined to (1) the tax/contribution module, (2) the generated return-data module, and (3) the UI's input sections, defaults and labels. The return generators, spending and rebalancing logic, and `src/metrics.py` stay shared (🔁).
*   **Swiss behaviour must not change**: every existing Swiss test, including the ESTV golden tests and the documented default success rates, must stay green throughout. Each milestone below lands with Swiss results bit-identical at the default seed. (M1 was verified bit-identical — `np.array_equal` on every output array — across six configurations covering all rebalancing and spending strategies, Pillar 2 / 3a payouts at 40–66, frozen and partial bracket indexation, and runs with and without an inflation matrix.)

### 7.4. Engine Changes (`run_simulation`) (cost basis ✅ M2, Austrian taxation ✅ M4)

#### 7.4.1. Cost-basis tracking (✅ M2, country-agnostic)
*   **State**: `cost_basis = liquid_assets.copy()` at Year 0, i.e. the whole starting portfolio is treated as bought on day 0 (zero unrealized gains; PRD decision 5). Sleeve indices are exported as `ASSET_US_STOCKS … ASSET_BITCOIN`; `NON_CASH_ASSETS = [US, Non-US, Gold, Bitcoin]` are the sleeves that can realize gains. Cash is always held at cost: `cost_basis[:, ASSET_CASH]` is re-synced to the cash balance every month (after the pension credit) and after the year-end rebalance, so interest, pension credits and debt never create gains.
*   **Helpers** (in `src/simulation_engine.py`, average-cost method = the Austrian "gleitender Durchschnittspreis"):

    ```python
    def _buy(liquid_assets, cost_basis, amounts)                        # basis += purchase price
    def _rebalance_to(liquid_assets, cost_basis, mask, target_values) -> realized
        # sells sleeves above target (average cost), buys sleeves below target, then ASSIGNS
        # liquid_assets[mask] = target_values so balances are bit-identical to a plain reallocation
    ```
*   **Where trades are booked**: Step 1 pension proceeds → `_buy`; Step 3 bankruptcy consolidation → positive non-cash holdings booked as sold (gain = value − basis) and all basis zeroed; Step 4.5 intra-year rebalance and Step 7 year-end rebalance (incl. the Cash Tent glide) → `_rebalance_to`; Step 6 proportional outflow sale → `_sell`; Step 6 dividends → in Switzerland `cost_basis[:, US/Non-US] += holding × dividend_yield` (the return series are total-return, so dividends are reinvested at market value); in Austria (`taxes_capital_gains`) they are **distributed**: `holding × dividend_yield` moves from each equity sleeve to cash (sleeve basis unchanged) and funds the outflow before any sale (`_cash_spent_first`: all positive cash for `cash_first` runs, otherwise the distributed cash, capped at the positive cash balance; `_outflow_sale_gain_profile(..., distributed_cash)` mirrors it, with `gain_fraction = unrealized / (positive total − cash spent first)`). Spending cash first (Smart Cash Buffer downturns, and Austrian runs whose year-end rebalance follows the sale) never realizes gains.
*   **Swiss results are unchanged**: realized gains are tracked but not taxed in CH, and every balance update is the same floating-point operation as before (verified bit-identical on all pre-existing output keys across the six M1 configurations).
*   **Outputs** (✅): `cost_basis_by_class` `shape=(duration_years, num_runs, 5)` (year-end, after rebalancing), `realized_gains` `shape=(duration_years, num_runs)` (sum over non-cash sleeves of the year's realized gains, negative for net losses), `unrealized_gains` `shape=(duration_years, num_runs)` (year-end `value − basis` summed over non-cash sleeves).
*   Tests: `tests/test_cost_basis.py` (helper arithmetic, zero returns never create gains, average-cost gain on the year-end sale, rebalancing realizes gains, dividend basis step-up (CH), bankruptcy zeroes the basis, output shapes); Austrian dividend distribution in `tests/test_austria_engine.py` (`test_dividends_are_distributed_and_fund_the_outflow`, `test_dividends_partly_cover_the_outflow_and_the_rest_is_sold`).

#### 7.4.2. Austrian taxation on top of the cost basis (✅ M4)
All four non-cash sleeves (US, Non-US, Gold ETC, Bitcoin) are taxable at 27.5%; cash interest at 25%. Only the Month-11 assessment and the after-tax watermark differ from the Swiss path; the Swiss path is untouched (all six M1 configurations still bit-identical).

**Monthly tick deltas vs. §3.3 (Austria mode)**:
1. *Start-of-year pension liquidations*: `Austria.liquidate_pensions` returns zeros (no Pillar 2 / 3a).
2. *Inflation & bracket indexation*: as in §3.3 step 2, with the Austrian deflation rule (`next_bracket_factors_skip_deflation`: negative inflation years leave the brackets unchanged). `bracket_factors` scale every euro amount of `tax_engine_at` (bracket edges, pensioner credit and its limits, the €620 allowance, €2,615 Freigrenze, § 67 bands, and the €6,930 Höchstbeitragsgrundlage) through `f × calculate_annual_taxes_at(x / f)`.
3. *Market returns*: unchanged (🔁).
4. *Monthly pension*: from 65, `monthly_ahv_pension × 14/12 × inflation_factor` gross is credited to EUR Cash every month (`pension_payments_per_year = 14`); `income_ahv` records the gross 14 payments.
5. *Intra-year rebalancing*: unchanged; `_rebalance_to` gains accumulate in `realized_gains_this_year` and are taxed at the year-end assessment.
6. *Annual taxation (Month 11)*, via `Austria.assess_annual_taxes`:
    * Taxable gains of the year = `carried_taxable_gains` (previous year-end rebalance) + non-cash gains realized so far (intra-year rebalances, bankruptcy consolidation) + the gains of the outflow sale.
    * `calculate_annual_taxes_at(pension, dividends, gains, interest)`: pension health contribution 6% (capped at the €6,930-per-payment Höchstbeitragsgrundlage); regular pension (12/14 of the gross, after the contribution) at the § 33 tariff minus the pensioner credit; 13th/14th payment under § 67 Abs. 1 (tax-free if two monthly gross payments ≤ the €2,615 Freigrenze, else 0% / 6% / 27% / 35.75% bands from €620, amounts above €83,333.33 at the tariff); capital pool `max(0, dividends + gains)` at 27.5% plus interest at 25%, or — if cheaper — pension + pool + interest all at the tariff (Regelbesteuerungsoption). Plus § 16 ASVG self-insurance `12 × health_insurance_monthly × inflation_factor × (1 + health_insurance_real_growth)^(age − start_age)` while `age < 65` or the pension is 0 (`Austria.health_self_insurance_annual`; the contribution follows the wage-based Höchstbeitragsgrundlage, default real growth 0.7%/yr = HBGL 2010–2026 +3.3%/yr vs Austrian CPI 2008–2024 +2.6%/yr).
    * **Outflow sale solved for its own tax**: `_outflow_sale_gain_profile` gives the gain fraction of the sale (proportional selling; `cash_first` runs — Smart Cash Buffer downturns and every run under `Monthly` / `Quarterly` / `Yearly` / `Cash Tent` rebalancing — spend cash first), its cash-first amount and the cap (total liquid wealth). Because $h(S) = E + T(S) - S$ is continuous and strictly decreasing for both capital gains ($0 \le g \le 1$, $\text{slope}(T) \le 0.55 < 1$) and capital losses ($g < 0$, $\text{slope}(T) \le 0$, where Picard fixed-point iteration oscillates into a 2-cycle whenever $|g \cdot \text{marginal\_rate}| > 1$), the engine brackets the unique root in $[E + \min(T(0), T(\text{cap})),\; E + \max(T(0), T(\text{cap}))]$ and runs `OUTFLOW_TAX_BISECTION_STEPS = 50` vectorized bisection steps ($2^{-50} \approx 9 \times 10^{-16}$), sells, then settles any tiny floating-point rounding residual on the actual realized gains against cash.
    * **Timing simplifications** (amounts unchanged): all KESt is settled at the year-end assessment instead of being withheld at each sale; the Regelbesteuerungsoption is applied in the same year instead of being refunded by the next year's assessment.
    * No wealth tax, AHV or capital withdrawal tax.
7. *Year-end rebalancing*: through `_rebalance_to`; its realized gains are stored in `carried_taxable_gains` and taxed in the next year's assessment (as if rebalancing on the first trading day of January). The final year's year-end rebalance gains are never taxed (end of horizon).

**Watermark** (✅): `jurisdiction.latent_tax(..., unassessed_gains)` returns the deferred capital withdrawal tax for CH and `0.275 × max(0, net unassessed gains)` for AT, where `_unassessed_gains = carried_taxable_gains + intra_year_realized_gains + unrealized_gains` so rebalancing before year-end KESt settlement never artificially inflates after-tax net worth (0 at Year 0, so `initial_net_worth_after_tax == initial_net_worth`).

**Ending net worth is gross of latent KESt** (by design, PRD §8.7): `net_worth` and everything derived from it — success mask, TL;DR verdict, ending-NW metrics, cohort tables — do not deduct the KESt still owed on unrealized gains or on the final year's carried rebalance gains; only the watermark does. At app defaults that latent tax is ~17% of the median ending net worth in Historic Backtesting (`0.275 × max(0, unrealized_gains[-1])` is available from the outputs if an after-tax view is ever needed).

**Year-0 estimate** (✅ `Austria.estimate_year_0_taxes_for_weights`): `calculate_annual_taxes_at` on Year-0 dividends, interest (at `cash_rate`) and the pension if `start_age >= 65`, plus 12 months of health self-insurance where due. It assumes the Year-0 outflow sale realizes no gain (the basis equals the value on day 0 and the Year-0 price moves are unknown at estimation time); the simulation itself taxes whatever gain that sale actually realizes. Drives Cash Tent sizing and Vanguard TWR sync like the Swiss estimate.

**Outputs** (✅): `tax_components` — a dict of `shape=(duration_years, num_runs)` arrays with the jurisdiction's components (CH: `income_tax`, `wealth_tax`, `ahv_contributions`; AT: `income_tax`, `kest`, `health_insurance`), summing to `taxes_paid` minus the Swiss capital withdrawal tax. `taxes_paid` remains the total of all taxes and mandatory contributions so the existing charts and metrics work unchanged; `income_ahv` holds the gross state pension in both countries (the planned rename to `income_state_pension` was dropped).

**Sanity check** (1,000 stationary-bootstrap runs, seed 42, 45 years, start 45, 2.5M, 80k expenses, Static spending, 2k/month pension, 50/30/10/5/5 allocation, 2% dividend yield, Yearly rebalancing, PPP 0%, success at 50%): CH success 90.2%, median lifetime real taxes 3.14M (income 1.36M, wealth 1.57M, AHV 0.20M); AT success 89.4%, 3.39M (KESt 3.07M, income tax 0.11M, health insurance 0.19M).

### 7.5. Return Data in EUR (✅ M3)
*   **Pipeline**: `scripts/fetch_source_data_at.py` (network; separate from the CHF fetch so the CHF sources are never touched) writes `data/usd_eur.csv`, `data/at_inflation.csv` and `data/at_cash_rate.csv`; `scripts/build_historic_returns.py --currency EUR` (offline) writes `src/historic_returns_eur.py`. The build script renders one parameterised template for both currencies; the CHF render is byte-identical to the previous `src/historic_returns.py` (verified by diff; only the build-date line changes). `--output PATH` writes elsewhere (used by the in-sync test).
*   EUR returns are built at index level like the CHF series: $\text{Return}^{\text{EUR}}_t = \frac{I^{\text{USD}}_t \cdot \text{FX}^{\text{EUR/USD}}_t}{I^{\text{USD}}_{t-1} \cdot \text{FX}^{\text{EUR/USD}}_{t-1}} - 1$.
*   **Sources** (FRED is unreachable from the build machine, so none are used):
    *   EUR/USD month-end, 1957-01+: BIS `WS_XRU` `M.AT.EUR.E` (schilling at the fixed 13.7603 ATS/EUR before 1999). Cross-checked against ECB daily reference-rate month-ends 1999+: identical (max diff 0.000%).
    *   Austrian CPI: Statistik Austria OGD `OGD_vpi58_VPI_1958_i_1` (official long-chained VPI 1958, 0.1 precision) from Dec 1959; BIS `WS_LONG_CPI` `M.AT.628` chained on before that (Dec 1957 / Dec 1958). The BIS monthly levels are too coarse for later years (2 decimals on a 2010 = 100 base, e.g. 1960: 0.0% vs VPI 0.8%); mean abs Dec/Dec difference 1960–2025 0.17pp, max 1.5pp (1976).
    *   EUR Cash 2000+: ECB `MIR` `M.AT.B.L22.F.R.A.2250.EUR.N` (Austrian households, deposits with agreed maturity ≤ 1 year, new business), annual mean; the notice-deposit (`L23`, gappy) and overnight (`L21`) rates are kept in the CSV for reference. Agreed-maturity vs notice deposits differ by 0.16pp on average over the 11 complete overlapping years.
*   **Composite construction** (PRD Q1, option b, and Q9):
    *   **1958–2025**: market EUR/USD, Austrian CPI; EUR Cash = Swiss savings rate translated under relative PPP, $(1 + r_{CH})(1 + \pi_{AT})/(1 + \pi_{CH}) - 1$, until 1999 and the ECB MIR rate from 2000.
    *   **1922–1957**: Swiss CPI chained onto the Austrian CPI at Dec 1957 (so every pre-1958 inflation rate *is* the Swiss rate); EUR/USD = Dec-1957 market rate × relative-PPP drift of Swiss vs US CPI, interpolated geometrically between Decembers; EUR Cash = Swiss savings rate as-is. This skips the 1921–22 hyperinflation and the 1945–47 reforms.
    *   `HISTORIC_REAL_EUR_APPRECIATION = 0.0057` is derived like the CHF constant over the **whole** 1922–2025 window. The 1922–1957 segment is PPP-neutral by construction, so the residual comes entirely from the market-rate years. The slider applies one uniform monthly adjustment to all 104 years (as in the CHF module), so a whole-window constant makes the 0% default PPP-neutral **on average over 1922–2025**: the market-rate years keep part of their real-FX drag (≈ 0.87%/yr over 1958–2025, offset by 0.57%) and the synthetic years gain +0.57%/yr. A constant derived over 1958–2025 only would make the market years exactly neutral but add a spurious +0.87%/yr to every pre-1958 year.

*   **Chart and table keys**: `render_results` runs once per engine, so every `st.plotly_chart` / `st.dataframe` carries a `key` derived from the column title (e.g. `"Monte Carlo_survival"`). Keyless elements are identified by their content, and two columns with identical charts (e.g. 100% survival everywhere at a 0% target) raised `StreamlitDuplicateElementId`.
